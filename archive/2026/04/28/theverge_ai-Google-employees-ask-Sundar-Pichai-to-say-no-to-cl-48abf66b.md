Over 600 Google employees signed a letter to CEO Sundar Pichai demanding that Google block the Pentagon from using its AI models for classified purposes, reports the [ The Washington Post](https://www.washingtonpost.com/technology/2026/04/27/google-employees-letter-ai-pentagon/). Its organizers claim many of the signers work in Google’s DeepMind AI lab, and include more than 20 principals, directors, and vice presidents.

According to the *Post*, the letter says that “The only way to guarantee that Google does not become associated with such harms is to reject any classified workloads. Otherwise, such uses may occur without our knowledge or the power to stop them.” Anthropic is currently in a legal battle with the Pentagon over being [designated a “supply chain risk,”](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/890347/pentagon-anthropic-supply-chain-risk) after refusing to loosen guardrails around how the US military can use its AI models, with support from across the tech industry, including [employees at Google](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/891514/anthropic-pentagon-lawsuit-amicus-brief-openai-google).

The letter specifically references a recent report by [ The Information](https://www.theinformation.com/articles/google-pentagon-discuss-classified-ai-deal-company-rebuilds-military-ties) that said Google and the Pentagon are

[discussing a deal](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/913269/google-is-reportedly-in-talks-to-let-the-pentagon-use-gemini-in-clasified-settings)for deploying its Gemini AI in classified settings. Microsoft

[already has deals to provide AI services in classified environments](https://www.theverge.com/2024/8/8/24216215/palantir-microsoft-azure-ai-defense-partnership-surveillance), and OpenAI
