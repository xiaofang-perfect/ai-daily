![A headshot of Australian Prime Minister Anthony Albanese](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/640/cpsprodpb/vivo/live/images/2026/9/24/2ec2c095-4845-4052-8037-03442eb147f4.jpg.webp) Image source, EPA

As discussions took place at the UN General Assembly in New York about the opportunities AI provides - and how best to manage it - Australian Prime Minister Anthony Albanese revealed that the country's universal health insurance scheme had been hacked by an OpenAI agent.

In fact, the hack happened in **June**, the company became aware of it in **August,** and then sent an email to a government agency's general inbox in **September**.

"It took the company way too long to inform the government," Albanese said, adding that the method of notifying officials was also "unacceptable".

Believed to be the first known breach of a government system by rogue AI agents, the hack accessed Medicare's statistics portal which contains private, but not sensitive, data.

Our cyber correspondent, Joe Tidy, writes that [experts believe the systems were poorly protected](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/live/cvgl73pxgndwt?post=asset%3A5d65dbad-8bab-432d-8a63-0f420744a3b0#post) - but more important is that this is not the first instance of AI agents ignoring laws on accessing online information.

Tech reporter Tom Gerken explains [AI itself has no true understanding of what it is being asked](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/live/cvgl73pxgndwt?post=asset%3Aa7747be0-0ee8-47b3-bc55-9ad50c9d4523#post) - while tech editor Zoe Kleinman asks if AI is in its ["move fast and break things"](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/live/cvgl73pxgndwt?post=asset%3A93e51023-21ba-4333-b6da-3d26dd1b07d0#post) era.

The Australian government has launched an review of AI laws and governance - but this is a global issue.

AI might have dominated discussions at Unga this week, but a consensus among world leaders on how best to harness opportunities while tackling the risks still feels a way off.
