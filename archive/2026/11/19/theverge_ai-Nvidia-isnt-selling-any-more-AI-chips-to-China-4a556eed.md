Nvidia has stopped sending its advanced AI chips to China despite the approval it received from the White House to do so [in December last year](https://www.theverge.com/news/840557/nvidia-h200-ai-chips-china-exports-us). The announcement was made by interim Nvidia CEO Colette Kress during an [earnings call on Tuesday](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-announces-financial-results-for-third-quarter-fiscal-2027), marking a notable shift for the company that, under former CEO Jensen Huang, fought for the right to ship its powerful Hopper architecture [H200 chips](https://www.theverge.com/2023/11/13/23958823/nvidia-h200-ai-gpu-announced-specs-release-date) to China.

# Nvidia isn’t selling any more AI chips to China

After former CEO Jensen Huang fought for US approval to sell H200 chips in China, Nvidia is now walking away.

“Our guidance does not have any licensed Hopper data center products included, and we are no longer shipping licenced Hopper data center products to China,” [Kress said](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-18/interim-nvidia-ceo-says-it-s-no-longer-sending-ai-chips-to-china). She did not explain why Nvidia was ceasing its H200 processor shipments or clarify whether Nvidia would pursue other business with China in the future.

The pivot comes after [Huang stepped down](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1030422/nvidia-jensen-huang-ai-china-alice-huang) from his position as Nvidia CEO last week following reports of multiple extramarital affairs, one with a woman who was suspected by US intelligence to be [a Chinese spy](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1029562/nvidia-investigating-jensen-huang-relationship-chinese-spy). Nvidia already faced difficulties around offloading the AI chips prior to this scandal, however. Concerns were raised about [failings to properly vet Chinese companies](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1029483/nvidia-ai-chips-china-blacklist-government) that were approved to handle the H200 AI chips. [*Reuters*](https://www.reuters.com/world/china/nvidia-chips-pile-up-hong-kong-china-withholds-import-approval-2026-11-05/) also reported earlier this month that around 600,000 H200 chips were stuck in Hong Kong after Beijing withheld approval for large-scale imports, with China seeking to reduce its dependency on US technology.
