# Title:How Well Does AI Peer Review Work? A Benchmark Built from 100 Planted Errors

[View PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.12820v1)

Abstract:One hundred known errors were planted into 10 open-access psychology papers, and the modified papers were then run through frontier models and two commercial AI review tools. The best single system caught 71 of the 100 errors, while the worst caught 30. Pooling every system's output caught 93. Models are only partly correlated in the errors they find, which makes ensembling a big lever for finding issues in papers. Seven errors could not be caught by any system, and all seven were omissions: information deleted from a paper rather than mistakes inserted into it. [this http URL](http://Refine.ink) contributes more unique catches than any other single system, though it is expensive on a per-error basis. False positives were not measured, and how this error distribution compares to the distribution of errors in real papers remains unknown. The papers, errors, model outputs, and full experiment log are public, so that others can build on this toward a comprehensive evaluation benchmark across disciplines.
