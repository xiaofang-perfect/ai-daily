The listing comes nearly a month after Anthropic revealed [its plans to go public](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1018476/anthropic-ipo-details-s1-filed-sec). In its lengthy S-1 filing, the AI company reported recording an $80.7 billion net loss in the first half of this year, most of which [comes from an accounting charge](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1021476/anthropic-s-1-compute-recursive#:~:text=Let%E2%80%99s%20start%20with,right%20now%2C%20yes.) that doesn’t reflect its business, along with $16.3 billion in revenue. The filing outlines plans that Anthropic says will help it develop AI for the benefit of humanity, which includes a governance structure that [keeps control of the company](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1019470/anthropic-ipo-resist-investor-pressure-cut-corners-safety) in the hands of the founders, rather than the shareholders.

While speaking ahead of Anthropic’s debut at the Nasdaq, CEO Dario Amodei highlighted his stance on AI safety. “To state it unequivocally, we will pace the training and deployment of AI systems, taking as long as is necessary to have confidence that they are safe and will remain under human control,” Amodei said, [according to *Reuters*](https://www.reuters.com/live/anthropic-ipo-live-claude-maker-goes-public-nasdaq-after-record-share-offering-2026-11-19/). “We will not bend to either business nor political pressure.”

Anthropic’s S-1 filing also [listed possible “catastrophic” risks](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1001838/anthropic-ipo-prospectus-ai-safety-threat) associated with its AI models, including “self-preserving behaviors” like resisting attempts to shut them down. It also warns potential shareholders that its AI models “may behave in unintended, misaligned, or autonomous ways” that can harm Anthropic.

Anthropic recently [revealed that, like rival OpenAI, it has experienced incidents of AI agents](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/994064/anthropic-spent-this-week-in-hot-water-over-cybersecurity) escaping their testing environment and [hacking third-party organizations](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/973670/anthropic-claude-hacked-organizations-during-cyber-tests). In September, Amodei [proposed a plan to slow down](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/994337/anthropic-ceo-slow-down-ai-development) the training and development of AI in response to the wave of rogue AI cyberattacks. As part of that plan, it gave safety evaluators at METR “employee-like access” to its internal systems, who found [that the company is using AI](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1029126/anthropic-metr-report-accelerating-ai-development) to accelerate its research and development.
