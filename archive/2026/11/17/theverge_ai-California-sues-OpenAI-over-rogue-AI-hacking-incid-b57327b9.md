California is suing OpenAI after its AI agents broke out of their testing environment and hacked other companies, including Hugging Face. In [a lawsuit filed Monday](https://www.documentcloud.org/documents/28912394-california-v-openai/), Attorney General [Rob Bonta accuses](https://oag.ca.gov/news/press-releases/it%E2%80%99s-golden-state-not-wild-west-attorney-general-bonta-sues-openai-over) OpenAI of violating consumer safety law by knowingly developing a “dangerous product without proper oversight,” [as reported earlier by *Reuters*](https://www.reuters.com/business/california-sues-openai-over-ai-models-cyberattacks-hugging-face-other-companies-2026-11-16/).

# California sues OpenAI over rogue AI hacking incidents

OpenAI lost control of its AI agents more than 80 times during testing, California’s AG claims.

The lawsuit claims the cyberattack on Hugging Face “was the predictable result of an internal culture at OpenAI that prioritizes growth and rapid model development over safety and accountability.” It alleges that OpenAI recently told the attorney general’s office that it “lost control” of its AI agents more than 80 times during testing.

After [revealing the rogue AI attack](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/968988/openai-hugging-face-hack-ai) on Hugging Face in July, news of several other similar incidents emerged, including attacks on an [Australian government website](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/999874/openai-agents-hacked-an-australian-government-website-in-search-for-data), [a German wiki site](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/990773/openai-german-wiki-incident), and the [software package manager RubyGems](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/994383/openais-rogue-ai-rubygems-hack). OpenAI’s agents [also tried to “bruteforce” a United Nations](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1001178/openai-agents-bruteforce-un-website) website.

“OpenAI’s actions escalated real-world cybersecurity risks, threatening the safety, health, and well-being of society,” Bonta says in the press release. “The company knowingly tested a dangerous product without proper oversight or safety procedures and misled the public about it — this disregard for public safety violated California law.”

OpenAI [released its new cybersecurity AI model earlier this month,](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1027599/openai-gpt-6-cyber-launch) saying that it’s less likely to go rogue and attack other platforms. It’s [also giving safety evaluators](https://www.apolloresearch.ai/blog/pilot-towards-embedded-evaluations) at Apollo Research access to its AI systems as part of a pilot program that monitors models for misalignment.

“Since the Hugging Face incident, we’ve undertaken an extensive and transparent effort to understand and address misaligned model activity,” OpenAI spokesperson Drew Pusateri says in an emailed statement to *The Verge*. “This lawsuit appears to single out OpenAI over a challenge that’s been identified by every major AI lab. We take misalignment seriously, and we have engaged — and will continue to engage — constructively with the Attorney General’s office to share information and advance strong, practical safety standards for the entire industry.”
