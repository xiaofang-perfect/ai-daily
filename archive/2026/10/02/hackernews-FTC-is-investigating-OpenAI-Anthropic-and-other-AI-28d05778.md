The Federal Trade Commission has opened an investigation into [OpenAI,](https://www.cnbc.com/quotes/OPENAI.FG/) [Anthropic](https://www.cnbc.com/quotes/ANTHR.FG/) and other [artificial intelligence](https://www.cnbc.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/) companies over the potential dangers posed by their products, an agency spokesperson confirmed to CNBC.

The probe adds to the mounting scrutiny that OpenAI and Anthropic have been facing over their safety practices, especially after industry researchers warned about how the companies' AI models could cause [catastrophic harm](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/09/anthropic-researcher-quits-ai-safety.html). OpenAI stunned the industry in July when it disclosed that its agents broke out of a testing environment and hacked into open-source platform [Hugging Face](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/22/open-ai-cyber-models-hack-hugging-face.html).

The FTC spokesperson declined to name any other companies that are being investigated. The [New York Post](https://nypost.com/2026/09/30/us-news/ftc-opens-sweeping-probe-of-anthropic-openai-and-other-super-intelligence-models/) was first to report the probe. 

Representatives for OpenAI and Anthropic did not immediately respond to CNBC's request for comment.

Earlier this month, Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei rocked the tech sector by urging AI companies to slow how quickly they improve their most advanced models and calling for stronger government oversight. He published a three-step proposal aimed at tempering the pace of development without "sacrificing commercial advantage or the United States' lead in AI."

Several of Amodei's competitors, including OpenAI CEO [Sam Altman](https://www.cnbc.com/sam-altman/) and SpaceX CEO [Elon Musk](https://www.cnbc.com/elon-musk/), expressed support for his proposal, while others, including [Meta](https://www.cnbc.com/quotes/META/) CEO [Mark Zuckerberg](https://www.cnbc.com/mark-zuckerberg/) and [Nvidia](https://www.cnbc.com/quotes/NVDA/) CEO Jensen Huang, argued that individual companies should be responsible for ensuring the safety of their products.

President [Donald Trump](https://www.cnbc.com/donald-trump/) convened top executives from [Alphabet](https://www.cnbc.com/quotes/GOOGL/), [Meta](https://www.cnbc.com/quotes/META/), [SpaceX](https://www.cnbc.com/quotes/SPCX/), Nvidia, [Palantir](https://www.cnbc.com/quotes/PLTR/), Anthropic, OpenAI and other companies on Tuesday to discuss the issue. The group signed a short voluntary, nonbinding accord, which says that "every company is responsible for developing its own technology safely and in a way that builds trust with customers and the public."

"We all need to work together to make sure that we can win, and we can win safely," Amodei said outside the White House on Tuesday. "If we do this right, if we work with the president and everyone here, we can win safely."
