The takeover was not entirely unexpected: SpaceX announced a [peculiar arrangement](https://www.theverge.com/science/916427/spacex-cursor-potential-deal-acquisition) in April in which it agreed to either acquire the programming platform for $60 billion or pay a $10 billion breakup fee. The company had been holding off completing the deal while going public.

In an [SEC filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1181412/000162828026043411/spaceexplorationtechnologi.htm), SpaceX said it expects the deal to close during the third quarter of 2026.

Musk has previously [expressed his frustration](https://www.ft.com/content/e5fbc6c2-d5a6-4b97-a105-6a96ea849de5?syn-25a6b1a6=1) with xAI’s sub-par coding product, which lags behind popular tools like [Anthropic’s Claude Code](https://www.theverge.com/report/874308/anthropic-claude-code-opus-hype-moment) and [OpenAI’s Codex](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/913034/openai-codex-updates-use-macos). Acquiring Cursor, which offers similar tools to automate coding, could help close the gap. The startup has grown explosively in recent years amid booming demand for more efficient programming tools and a shift toward “[vibe coding](https://www.theverge.com/tech/928905/vibe-code-personal-software-revolution)” in the industry.
