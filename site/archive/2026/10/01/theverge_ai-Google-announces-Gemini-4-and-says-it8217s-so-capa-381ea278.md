Google today revealed its next AI frontier model, which it’s calling Gemini 4 Argon. The new model delivers “frontier performance in complex workflows across real-world software engineering, enterprise knowledge work like legal and finance, and cybersecurity defense,” [according to](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/gemini-4-argon/) chief AI architect and Google DeepMind SVP Koray Kavukcuoglu. But the company is limiting access at first to a “set of trusted cyber defenders,” and Kavukcuoglu says that Google is “actively engaged in the U.S. government’s voluntary process for pre-release model access while we gradually expand access.”

# Google announces Gemini 4 and says it’s so capable that only ‘trusted cyber defenders’ can have it right now

Gemini 4 Argon is launching first in a limited capacity so Google can make sure it’s not misaligned.

Kavukcuoglu says Gemini 4 Argon is already powering Google’s “internal workflows,” highlighting how the model has helped with tasks like large-scale codebase migrations. Google also shared a big chart showing many benchmarks where Gemini 4 fares better than competing models from OpenAI and Anthropic. (The AI community was buzzing earlier this week on X over what appeared to be [leaked](https://x.com/rynorhn/status/2104497579463786839) [benchmarks](https://x.com/JustLingonberry/status/2104431004337479933) for the new model.) Google will be strengthening “critical frontier safeguards” before a broader rollout, including defending against misuse and prompt injection attacks and monitoring for misalignment.

The launch of Gemini 4 Argon comes just a day after [OpenAI’s splashy DevDay conference](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1001681/openai-devday-2026-biggest-news-announcements), where OpenAI launched its [Dots AI agent](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1002033/openai-dots-launch-muse-competitor) and GPT-6.1 Sol AI model. (OpenAI also said this week that it wouldn’t be releasing a planned GPT-6.1 Astra model due to [worries about safety](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1001799/openai-wont-release-gpt-6-1-astra-due-to-worries-about-safety).) It’s also arriving soon after Kavukcuoglu’s appointment as the new boss of DeepMind [in August](https://www.theverge.com/tech/975677/google-deepmind-ai-demis-hassabis-shakeup).
