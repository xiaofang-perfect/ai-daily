Shares of [Nvidia](https://www.cnbc.com/quotes/NVDA/), [Oracle](https://www.cnbc.com/quotes/ORCL/), [CoreWeave](https://www.cnbc.com/quotes/CRWV/) and other [artificial intelligence](https://www.cnbc.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/) names sank lower on Thursday after the market learned more details about OpenAI's revenue.

[OpenAI](https://www.cnbc.com/quotes/OPENAI.FG/) told investors that it hit roughly $50 billion in [annualized revenue](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/29/openai-devday-2026-live-updates.html) at the end of September, CNBC confirmed, lower than the the $68 billion figure that was widely reported late last month. A person familiar with the matter said the $68 billion figure included gross revenue from OpenAI's partners, which helps investors make a more direct comparison with its chief rival, [Anthropic](https://www.cnbc.com/quotes/ANTHR.FG/).

The [Financial Times](https://www.ft.com/content/b66a9858-f8fb-46cb-b506-44bfe26fca2a?syn-25a6b1a6=1) was first to report the $50 billion figure.

OpenAI shared an update about its finances in an investor presentation, said the person, who asked not to be named in order to discuss the numbers. In addition to the $50 billion in annualized revenue, OpenAI touted 77% total run rate growth during its third quarter, as well as 107% run rate growth for its enterprise business during the same period, the person said.

Nvidia shares fell 3%, Oracle shares fell nearly 6% and CoreWeave shares slipped nearly 8% on Thursday. Additionally, [Advanced Micro Devices](https://www.cnbc.com/quotes/AMD/) fell 4%, [Broadcom](https://www.cnbc.com/quotes/AVGO/) fell 4%, [Intel](https://www.cnbc.com/quotes/INTC/) fell 5% and [Super Micro Computer](https://www.cnbc.com/quotes/SMCI/) fell nearly 5%.

OpenAI is under pressure to justify its $852 billion valuation to investors as it gears up for what is widely expected to be a blockbuster IPO. OpenAI [confidentially filed](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/06/08/openai-confidentially-files-for-ipo-prepping-wall-street-for-ai-debut.html) its prospectus with regulators in June, and executives have signaled that the company is [eyeing a 2027 debut](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/19/open-ai-ipo-timing-2027-friar.html).

Anthropic is also [readying for a major IPO](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/06/01/anthropic-ipo-s1-prospectus.html). The company has not officially disclosed when it plans to debut, but it's been [engaging in meetings](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/13/anthropic-cfo-early-ipo-meetings-valuation.html) with prospective investors and is reportedly seeking a $2 trillion valuation. In August, Anthropic told investors that its annualized revenue run rate hit [$65 billion](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/17/anthropic-says-annualized-revenue-climbed-to-65-billion-in-july.html) at the end of July.

In [a report](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/07/anthropic-will-be-most-ridiculous-ipo-of-year-analyst-says.html) on Tuesday, independent financial research provider New Constructs called Anthropic's upcoming offering the "most ridiculous IPO of 2026," and valued the company at a mere $150 billion. Anthropic's revenue in 2025 was $4.6 billion as the company racked up a net loss of $42 billion, according to [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/finance/anthropics-ipo-prospectus-shows-sweeping-ai-vision-surging-costs-2026-09-28/), which cited a leaked copy of the company's prospectus.

Both Anthropic and OpenAI have been at the center of a fierce debate over AI safety, after a growing chorus of researchers warned that the companies' models could potentially cause [catastrophic harm.](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/09/anthropic-researcher-quits-ai-safety.html) OpenAI has disclosed [several incidents](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/26/openai-agent-model-behavior-review.html) where its models behaved in unintended ways, and the company recently [pulled its plans](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/28/openai-abandons-plan-to-release-upcoming-model-as-safety-concerns-escalate.html) to launch GPT-6.1 Astra, saying the model did not meet its safety standards.

OpenAI CEO [Sam Altman](https://www.cnbc.com/sam-altman/) said in September that "right now would be an [ill-advised moment](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/13/china-dilemma-ai-slowdown-anthropic.html) to go public," in part because of the ongoing concerns around safety.

As OpenAI bides its time, the company is engaging in [early stage discussions](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/29/openai-devday-2026-live-updates.html) with investors about a potential new funding round. The company could raise around $30 billion, CNBC previously reported, but that figure could change. The round is being driven by investor demand and no term sheet has been finalized yet.

OpenAI closed a historic [$122 billion](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/03/31/openai-funding-round-ipo.html) funding round in March, and CFO Sarah Friar told CNBC last week that it is still "very well capitalized."
