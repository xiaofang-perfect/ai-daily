USA Today Co., along with the several local newspapers it owns, is suing OpenAI over claims that the company copied “hundreds of thousands” of articles to train its AI models, [as reported earlier by *Reuters*](https://www.reuters.com/legal/legalindustry/usa-today-sues-openai-copyright-infringement-over-ai-training-2026-10-08/). In [a filing on Thursday](https://www.documentcloud.org/documents/28731453-usa-today-v-openai/), the publisher asks for damages of more than $250 million, alleging OpenAI’s unauthorized use of its content “has done real and continuing” harm to its outlets.

# USA Today becomes the latest publisher to sue OpenAI

The publisher is seeking damages of more than $250 million.

This is just the latest in a string of copyright lawsuits filed against OpenAI. In [addition to a copyright lawsuit filed by *The New York Times*](https://www.theverge.com/2023/12/27/24016212/new-york-times-openai-microsoft-lawsuit-copyright-infringement), OpenAI is also facing [legal action from *The Intercept*](https://www.theverge.com/2024/2/28/24085973/intercept-raw-story-alternet-openai-lawsuit-copyright), [CNET owner Ziff Davis](https://www.theverge.com/news/656044/ziff-davis-sues-openai-ign-cnet-pcmag), [CBC/Radio-Canada](https://www.theverge.com/2024/11/29/24308984/openai-sued-canadian-media-copyright-chatgpt), [Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/895372/encyclopedia-britannica-openai-lawsuit), Merriam-Webster, [*The Seattle Times*](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/990932/seattle-times-newsday-lawsuit-openai-microsoft), and a coalition of [nearly 400 local newspapers](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/956543/nearly-400-local-newspapers-are-suing-openai-and-microsoft).

“OpenAI’s commercial success rests on large-scale copyright infringement,” USA Today Co.’s lawsuit claims. It alleges that OpenAI never asked for permission to use its content, instead “took” *USA Today*’s content to “build products worth hundreds of billions of dollars.” *The Tennessean*, *Indy Star*, *The Columbus Dispatch*, *The Oklahoman*, and several other local outlets are also included in the lawsuit.

OpenAI didn’t immediately respond to *The Verge*’s request for comment.
