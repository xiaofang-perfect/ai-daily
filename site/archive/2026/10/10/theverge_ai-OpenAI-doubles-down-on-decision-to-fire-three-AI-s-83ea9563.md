OpenAI is standing firm on its decision to fire three safety researchers after an investigation found they committed “a significant breach of trust.”

# OpenAI doubles down on decision to fire three AI safety researchers

The AI lab insisted the decision was about a ‘breach of trust,’ not raising safety issues.

In a [post](https://x.com/OpenAINewsroom/status/2108441580806025712) on X on Friday, the company said Jasmine Wang, Tomek Korbak and Mikita Balesni were dismissed for violating “clear policies on handling sensitive information.” It insisted the decision was not about the trio speaking out about the company and their concerns about AI safety.

The post is a direct response to an open [letter](https://mikitabalesni.com/letter/letter.pdf) the researchers published on Thursday urging OpenAI to be more transparent about the decision. In it and a [series](https://x.com/j_asminewang/status/2108263312291180680?s=20) of [social](https://x.com/tomekkorbak/status/2108266859397283953?s=20) [media](https://x.com/balesni/status/2108262814003687745?s=20) posts, the group said they believed they were fired for raising safety concerns and had “acted in line with OpenAI’s mission and within the working norms of the time.”

OpenAI said an internal investigation uncovered breaches “beyond what’s outlined in the letter,” but did not provide details.

The [safety of frontier systems](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/972380/open-ai-hugging-face-hack-ai-safety-warning) has become an increasing [concern](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/996563/ai-safety-research-metr-redwood-openai-anthropic) for staff inside AI companies following a series of [high profile breaches](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1000644/irregular-rogue-ai-cyberattacks-hacking-openai-meta-anthropic-google) this year, [most notably OpenAI’s hack of Hugging Face](https://www.theverge.com/column/980337/rogue-ai-science-fiction-openai). Many staff are now calling for companies to slow down and [do more to guard against](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/999881/why-cant-we-airgap-rogue-ai-agents) the potential consequences of building self-improving systems.
