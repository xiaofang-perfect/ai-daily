In [an announcement on Wednesday](https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DOC-427572A1.pdf), the FCC says it authorized SpaceX to deploy up to 1 million AI chip-equipped satellites as part of the Elon Musk-owned [company’s “Starmind” initiative](https://www.spacex.com/spacexsi/starmind). With [several others also pursuing the idea of putting AI data centers in space](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/845453/space-data-centers-astronomers), including Google and Blue Origin, this is the first approval the agency has granted for a full “constellation” of AI satellites.

# FCC signs off on SpaceX’s plan to put up to 1 million data center satellites into space

SpaceX aims to power AI capabilities here on Earth with its ‘Starmind’ constellation.

[The FCC’s approval requires](https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DA-26-1273A1.pdf) SpaceX to deploy at least one satellite by 2033, and meet the 1 million satellite milestone by 2040. The FCC also deferred SpaceX’s request to dispose of satellites located above 600km in [space “graveyard” orbits](https://www.nesdis.noaa.gov/news/graveyard-orbits-and-the-satellite-afterlife), rather than having them burn up in the Earth’s atmosphere, saying the company hasn’t provided “sufficient information” about its disposal process plans.

SpaceX has [described](https://www.spacex.com/spacexsi/starmind) its Starmind project as a way to “capture solar energy in space to power” AI capabilities on Earth. The company says Starmind will transmit data to Earth using high-speed lasers that connect to Starlink. It plans to produce chips for its satellites at an [upcoming Terafab facility](https://www.theverge.com/transportation/907976/elon-musk-terafab-intel-ai-chip-spacex-tesla).

Google has already taken its first steps toward putting compute into space, with the [launch of a “Project Suncatcher” AI server](https://www.theverge.com/tech/1023528/google-project-suncatcher-ai-satellite-transmission) satellite last month. Jeff Bezos’ [Blue Origin will carry out](https://www.theverge.com/tech/1037535/google-blue-origin-project-suncatcher-2029) the first dedicated heavy-lift launch for Project Suncatcher in 2029.
