Google has pulled the plug on Project Mariner, an experimental feature designed to perform tasks for you across the web, as [reported earlier by ](https://x.com/ZeffMax/status/2051824497242833234?s=20)*Wired*’s Maxwell Zeff. The [Project Mariner landing page](https://labs.google.com/mariner/landing) now contains a message that says: “Thank you for using Project Mariner. It was shut down on May 4th, 2026 and its technology voyaged to other Google products.”

Google [first revealed Project Mariner](/2024/12/11/24317436/google-deepmind-project-astra-mariner-ai-agent) in December 2024 and later announced an update allowing it to [perform up to 10 tasks at a time.](/google/671200/google-googling-ai-mode-project-mariner-i-o-2025) Over the past year, Google has integrated features powered by Project Mariner into its other AI tools, including [Gemini Agent](/news/670848/google-agent-mode-gemini-app-project-mariner-i-o-2025), which can do things like archive emails on your behalf or help you book a hotel. Google also folded Project Mariner’s agentic capabilities into its [AI-powered search feature](/google-io/670439/google-ai-mode-search-io-2025), AI Mode.

Earlier this year, Google showed off a new AI-powered feature, called “auto-browse,” that can perform multi-step tasks in Chrome, like researching flight costs. Though Google doesn’t say whether it’s powered by Project Mariner, it seems like a response to the agentic web browsing tools offered by [OpenAI](/2025/1/23/24350395/openai-chatgpt-operator-agent-control-computer), [Perplexity](/news/709025/perplexity-comet-ai-browser-chrome-competitor), and OpenClaw.

With the removal of Project Mariner, Google may be making room for the other AI features that could make their debut during this year’s I/O, [which starts on May 19th](/tech/880401/google-io-2026-dates-ai). Google didn’t immediately respond to *The Verge*’s request for comment.
